# Changelog

All notable changes to the npm package `wallhammer-web` are recorded in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the package follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). While the version is 0.x, a minor version (for example 0.4 to 0.5) may contain breaking changes. A patch version does not.

## Unreleased

## 0.8.3

### Added

- **Loads on a wheeled body** (in `world`): `WheeledBody.setLoad(slot, mass, x?, y?, z?)`, `clearLoads()`, `loadMass`, `totalMass` and `WHEELED_LOAD_SLOTS` (8): point masses fixed at points of the body's frame. A loaded body steps about the common centre of mass, so it rests lower, leans toward a load off its centre, pitches and rolls more under a high one and gathers speed more slowly; `position` and `velocity` stay those of the body's own centre, `restHeight` and `kineticEnergy` count the loads. Setting a load to what it already is does not wake a sleeping body. A body without loads steps bit for bit as before. See [Loads and forces](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/wheeled.md#loads-and-forces).
- **A force on a wheeled body** (in `world`): `WheeledBody.applyForce(px, py, pz, fx, fy, fz)` applies a force (N, world) at a world point for the next step only; several calls add up and it wakes the body. `WheeledBody.advance(dt, intent, afterStep?)` calls `afterStep` after every fixed step, the place to ask for the force again.
- **Saved state of a group of wheeled bodies** (in `world`): `WheeledGroup.stateBytes`, `saveState(out?, offset?)`, `loadState(bytes, offset?)` and `simState(name)`: the group's leftover time and every body's state in the order of `bodies`.
- **Loads in the examples**: in the movement course the seated figure is a load of 80 kg at the car's seat, rising as the figure sits down (the car sinks 7.5 mm and leans 0.3° to the driver's side), and `L` puts a roof load on the driven car: a rack with three crates and a strapped bundle (`compactRoofLoadMesh`, `COMPACT_ROOF_LOAD` in `examples/shared/src/vehicles/compact.ts`), 180 kg at 1.66 m, with which the car rests 17 mm lower, covers 1.53 m instead of 1.75 m in its first second and rolls 7.2° instead of 6.1° in the same turn. Hash `roof=0`, presets `car-load-stand`, `car-load-accelerate` and `car-load-turn`, `__wh.roofLoad(on?, car?)`. The glider's status line says why getting off is refused: in the air, moving, or the spots behind the wings too near the course's edge (`gliding().offRefused`).

### Changed

- **The saved state of a `WheeledBody` is version 2** (in `world`): it carries the loads and is 1,136 bytes with four wheels (was 880); `loadState` still reads a state of the old format, also as a part of a `Simulation` snapshot written before. Size a buffer from `stateBytes`.
- **The marks panel asks in the panel** (in `platform`): `installMarking` no longer opens a dialog of the browser (`window.confirm`, `window.prompt`), which blocked a page driven by a script. `reopen` and `wontfix` put a text field for the reason into the mark's row, or into the bar of the comparison when asked there (Enter saves, also with no text; Escape drops it; the typed text survives the panel's poll of the store); `×` turns into `delete` and `keep` in the row before a mark is removed. The records, the store and `MarkingApi` are unchanged.

### Fixed

- **`marks=` stays in the address of the examples**: `endless`, `outpost`, `fantasy-village`, `planet` and `system` rewrite their hash as the view moves and dropped `marks=` from it, so a reload of a page opened with `#marks=1` (or `marks=0`) lost the choice. They keep it now; the exact hash a mark records is unchanged (it never carried `marks=`).
- **The compact car's roof in the examples**: its right half was drawn as glass; both halves are paint.
- **The movement example's HUD while driving**: its position line showed the place of the parked figure and "in the air"; it shows the driven car's place and speed.

## 0.8.2

Version 0.8.1 was never published to npm; its changes ship here.

### Added

- **A body carried by another** (in `motion`): `SocketSeat` (type `SocketSeatOptions`) gives the root of a body carried at a socket of another, eased from the root the game gives it (weight 0) to the socket's transform (weight 1), with a share `tilt` of the socket's tilt (default 1, the whole of it; 0 keeps the carried body upright, `uprightTransform`); handedness is kept. `TwoBoneReach.setTargetSocket(world, socket)` sets a reach's target and end rotation from a socket (existing calls unchanged). See [A body carried by another](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/motion.md#a-body-carried-by-another).
- **Steps of several legs** (in `motion`): `StepCycle` (types `StepCycleDesc`, `StepPattern`), a cycle of steps driven by the distance travelled: patterns as data (per-leg offset, duty and lift, a stride, a speed) blended by speed with the offsets on a circle, planted ends that do not move until lifted, swings on an arc to a predicted landing on `heightAt`, a support guard, a shuffle home when standing, a hastened cycle when an end would be left beyond `reach`; per leg `targets`, `planted`, `swing`, `landed`, `downs`, for the body `supportHeight`, `pitch`, `roll`; `reset`, `rebase` for a new chart, `save`/`restore`. Not in fixed steps, the same places at 30, 60 and 144 Hz within a few centimetres; deterministic, no allocation per frame. `pnpm check:motion` checks the seat, the socket reach and the steps (held ends, no slide, offsets, blends, a turn on the spot, a stop mid-stride, frame rates, bits, save and restore, the support plane, a change of chart, a body of four legs reaching its targets). See [Steps of several legs](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/motion.md#steps-of-several-legs).
- **A wheeled body** (in `world`): `WheeledBody` (types `WheeledBodySetup`, `WheeledIntent`, `WheelSpec`, `WheeledTuning`, `FourWheelsOptions`; `DEFAULT_WHEELED_BODY`, `fourWheels`, `newWheeledIntent`, `WheelState`, `WHEELED_TUNING_KEYS`, `WHEELED_OBSTACLE_TAG`), one rigid body on wheel rays over a `CollisionWorld` in fixed steps: springs and dampers, tyres with a slip curve, load sensitivity and a friction circle, drive to a top speed, brakes, a handbrake, steering reduced with speed, a hull of spheres that hits the ground and solids without passing through thin walls, sleep on slopes, `retune`, `teleport(x, y, z, yaw?)` (still, the heading kept without a yaw, as for the walker), `setUpright` after a roll, `saveState`/`loadState`/`simState` as for the walker, drawing with `renderMatrix`, `wheelMatrix` and `renderYaw`. `syncObstacle` keeps a box round the hull so walkers are blocked; `WheeledGroup` and `collideWheeledBodies` let bodies meet (a body in a group draws at the group's `alpha` by default and is not advanced on its own). `CollisionWorld.sphereContacts` (with `newSphereContacts`, type `SphereContacts`) and `CollisionWorld.setBox` are new queries it uses. `pnpm check:walk` checks rest, slopes, acceleration, braking, turning circles, a handbrake turn, a ramp, a kerb, a jump, walls, rolling over, energy, a walker against a parked body, two bodies, frame rates, bits, save and restore and the time per step. See [Wheeled body](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/wheeled.md).
- **A flying body** (in `world`): `FlyingBody` (types `FlyingBodySetup`, `FlyingIntent`; `DEFAULT_FLYING_BODY`, `newFlyingIntent`, `FLYING_BODY_STATE_BYTES`), a body that crawls on the ground and flies over a `CollisionWorld` in fixed steps: a crawl along its heading with a leap off the ground; in the air a glider of one lift coefficient (lift and drag from a trim speed and a glide ratio, pitch, banked turns with a load limit, a stall, beats that push, a flare, path damping, a ceiling), touch-downs and perching; a hull of spheres (and one for the ground) against the ground and every solid without passing through thin walls; every number a setup field; drawing with `renderMatrix`, `renderPitch`, `renderBank`, `renderBeatPhase`; save and restore, `simState`. Deterministic, no allocation per step. `pnpm check:walk` checks the take-off, the glide's sink rate, a stall and the dive out of it, a banked turn's radius, a touch-down, a wall and the ground, perching, bits, save and restore. See [Flying body](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/flying.md).
- **A flight assist** (in `world`): `FlightAssist` (types `FlightAssistSetup`, `FlightAssistInput`; `DEFAULT_FLIGHT_ASSIST`, `newFlightAssistInput`) flies a `FlyingBody` from a wanted speed moved by a throttle, a climb or descent and a turn: it writes the body's intent before every fixed step by inverting the body's own model, so hands off it holds its speed and height, reaches a new speed in about 1.5–2.5 s, holds climb and descent rates, turns level without losing speed, never stalls, lands from a slow descent and, held against a solid in the air, eases off (`blocked`) so the body slides off or lands; every number a setup field. `FlyingBody` gains `softBeatRate` (slower light beats), `fastDrag` (a top speed) and a `prepare` hook in `advance`, all off by default (the existing scenarios are bit-equal). A perched `FlyingBody` that levels out now sinks with the levelling onto its hold instead of hopping off it (one touch-down where there were several; `flying-perch-on-roof` now perches once at the roof's height). The movement example's glider is flown through it by default (W/S faster and slower, A/D turn, Space climb, Shift descend; `assist=0` keeps the raw controls), with swept wings at speed, a closer camera in the air, open land round the course that the glider flies over (the fence stops it only on the ground; a soft turn back keeps it within reach, and it lands only on the course). `pnpm check:walk` checks it (`flying-assist-*`, `glide-assist-*`). See [Flying body](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/flying.md#flight-assist).
- **Gliding in the examples**: the movement course has a glider to fly, a huge flying animal with the build of a ray (a short broad body, a big round head, long swept wings each one thick fleshy fin that bends along its span, fat four-toed paws at the wings' front corners, short pelvic fins with smaller paws, two long tails), the rider small on a fringed diamond rug, a rope harness with a halter and reins, a lantern under the chin, a bedroll and bags (`examples/shared/src/figures/glider.ts`, `glider-gait.ts`, `membrane.ts`, `examples/movement/src/glide.ts`): a `FlyingBody` that crawls on four paws stepped by `StepCycle`, crouches and leaps off with the nose thrown up and deep first beats, beats, glides, banks, flares (the wings cupped, the paws reaching down) and lands deep on its paws; getting on and off with key `E` behind its left wing; presets `glide-*`; checked in `pnpm check:walk`.
- **Riding in the examples**: the movement course (the example `movement`) has a steed to ride, a slender long-legged runner with leaf feathers, a woven rug, a rope harness and a lantern (`examples/shared/src/figures/steed.ts`, `quad-gait.ts`): four legs stepped by `StepCycle` in a walk, a trot and a rotary gallop with a gathered and a stretched flight, the rider on a `SocketSeat` with hands and feet held at sockets, getting on and off with key `E`; checked in `pnpm check:walk`.
- **Driving in the examples**: the movement course has two compact cars to drive (`examples/shared/src/vehicles/compact.ts`, `examples/movement/src/car.ts`): each a `WheeledBody`, the two stepped in a `WheeledGroup` with `syncObstacle`, wheels that turn, steer and ride their springs, a body that dives and rolls, brake and reversing lamps, tyre marks, a longer camera eased behind the car, getting in and out with key `E` through an opening door; checked in `pnpm check:walk`.
- **Slopes and the walker's speed** (in `world`): `WalkerSetup.uphillSpeedLoss`, `downhillSpeedGain`, `downhillSpeedLoss` and `slopeSpeedFloor` scale the wanted speed on the ground by the grade of the ground along the wanted direction (1 − loss · g uphill, 1 + d · (gain − loss · d) at downhill grade d, never below the floor): slower uphill and on steep descents, a little faster on gentle ones. The grade is probed over a metre ahead and behind on the base and on solid tops (stairs read as their mean slope) and eased over 0.3 s; nothing is scaled in the air. The three rates default to 0 (`slopeSpeedFloor` 0.25) and the default setup steps bit for bit as before; the eased grade takes the spare double of the saved state, which stays 160 bytes. All four are in `WalkerTuning` and `WALKER_TUNING_KEYS`, so an object typed `WalkerTuning` must now name them. The examples' movement profiles and the movement course's steed use them. `pnpm check:walk` checks the defaults, the speeds up and down ramps, the floor, the air, stairs against a ramp and bits. See [Slopes and speed](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#slopes-and-speed).
- **Framing and a heading to follow for `FollowRig`** (in `camera`): `extraBoom` and `extraPivot` (metres, 0 by default) add the game's own framing to the user's `distance` and to `pivotHeight`, kept apart from the user's zoom so taking them away gives the user's boom back exactly (`wantedBoom` is the sum, within the limits, 0 in first person, where only the pivot extra applies); `state()` keeps the user's `distance` and leaves the extras out, so saved views load as before. `followHeading(yawDeg | null)` eases the yaw toward a heading, the short way round, 95 % in `FollowSetup.headingLag` seconds (default 1), waiting `headingPause` seconds (default 1) after the user turns the view (`turnPixels`, which `bindWalkInput` calls); off by default. Both new setup fields are optional, and a rig that uses none of this draws bit for bit as before. `pnpm check:follow` checks the defaults, the extras on and off, the easing, the pause and the wrap at ±180°. See [`FollowRig`](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/cameras.md#followrig).
- **The grade probe of the walker is set up** (in `world`): `WalkerSetup.gradeProbe` (metres ahead and behind, default 1) and `gradeSmoothing` (seconds, default 0.3), optional and fixed at construction, so a longer body reads the slope over a window of its size; `Walker.grade` reads the eased grade (read only). The defaults step bit for bit as before (`walker-slope-probe-window`). See [Slopes and speed](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#slopes-and-speed).
- **A contact trail** (in `core` and `render`): `ContactTrail` (type `ContactMark`), marks where something touches a surface (place, normal, heading, size, the game's `kind`, the time laid, a life) in a ring of fixed capacity (`CONTACT_TRAIL_FLOATS` a mark) with the changed span for the upload; `contactTrailFade` and `CONTACT_TRAIL_GLSL`, the fade with age on the CPU and in GLSL (full strength over the first `CONTACT_TRAIL_FADE_FROM` of the life); `ContactStreaks` (type `ContactStreakOptions`), chains of marks from continuous contacts. `ContactTrailLayer` (type `ContactTrailLayerOptions`) draws the ring in one instanced draw with the game's `trailShade` through `contactTrailMaterial` (type `ContactTrailMaterialOptions`; `CONTACT_TRAIL_ATTRIBS`, `CONTACT_TRAIL_DEFAULT_SHADE` the default look). See [Contact trail](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#contact-trail).
- **Particle bursts fired once** (in `gen-core` and `render`): `ParticleLayer.fire(burst)` with a `ParticleBurst` (seeded, the same seed the same particles), simulated in the vertex shader from `burstTime`, a clock the game sets; `burstsBusy`, `burstStats` (type `ParticleBurstStats`), `clearBursts`, option `burstCapacity`; the ring itself is `ParticleBurstRing` (with `PARTICLE_FLOATS`) in `gen-core`. Looping sources draw as before. See [Particles](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#particles).
- **Surface tags of the ground** (in `world`): the option `CollisionWorld` `baseTagAt(x, z)` gives the base surface a tag; `groundTagAt` tells the tag of the ground `groundAt` finds (a solid's top or the base), `baseTagAt` the base's alone; a ray hit on the base reports the base's tag (−1 without the option, as before); `WheelState.surfaceTag` is the tag under each wheel. See [Surface tags](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#surface-tags).
- **Prints, ruts and dust in the movement example**: boot prints from the figure's step events, hoof prints from the steed's step cycle, ruts and skid marks from the cars' wheels on one `ContactTrail` (the cars' own ring of tyre marks is gone), dust bursts from hooves at a trot or a gallop and from wheels on loose ground or sliding, a patch of loose ground in front of the start; presets `trace-trot`, `trace-gallop`, `trace-car`, `trace-handbrake`. `pnpm check:walk` checks the ring, the fade, the chains, the bursts' bits, the tags and ruts on the loose patch.
- **The step thresholds of `WheeledBody` are set up** (in `world`): `WheeledBodySetup.bounceSpeed` (1 m/s), `bumpStopFactor` (10), `wheelSphereShare` (0.8), `reverseEngageSpeed` (1 m/s) and `uprightProbeHeight` (1 m, how far above the centre of mass `setUpright` looks down for the ground). `DEFAULT_WHEELED_BODY` and the options of `fourWheels` are documented as a reference set that a game replaces with its own body's numbers; the airborne spin decay stays a known constant per step. See [Tuning](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/wheeled.md#tuning).
- **A seat timeline** (in `motion` and `world`): `SeatTransition` (types `SeatTransitionOptions`, `SeatTransitionPhase`, `SeatPhase`; `SEAT_TRANSITION_STATE_BYTES`), the whole of getting onto a seat and off it with the caller's numbers: an approach from where the body stood, a seat weight and a rise of the free root eased analytically per phase, cues at times (`passed`, `crossed`, `became`), the free place while it moves (`place`), the blend with the socket (`write`, through the `SocketSeat` it owns), `progress` for a camera, `save`/`restore` of a state of 88 bytes (as for `MotionGraph` and `StepCycle`). `headingRoot` makes the root of a body whose skeleton faces +z. In `world`, `attachFromWalker` starts it from a walker and `releaseToWalker` puts the walker at once where the body will stand as getting off begins. `pnpm check:motion` and `pnpm check:walk` check the phases, cues at two frame rates, the places, save and restore and the walker's place. See [Getting on and off: a seat timeline](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/motion.md#getting-on-and-off-a-seat-timeline).
- **Free places to stand** (in `world`): `CollisionWorld.standSpot(sx, sz, tx, tz, y, options)` (type `StandSpotOptions`) tells whether a body may stand at a place it comes to from a start (nothing in the way, the ground within a rise) and returns the ground height or NaN; `firstStandSpot(spots, y, options, out?)` returns the first free of several candidates. No allocation. `pnpm check:walk` checks a wall, a pit, a low and a high step. See [Free places to stand](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#free-places-to-stand).
- **Fixed steps out of frame times** (in `core`): `FixedSteps`, the accumulator of a fixed-rate body (`advance(dt, step, maxSteps?)`, `alpha`, `reset`, `leftover`), now behind `Walker`, `WheeledBody`, `WheeledGroup` and `ChartWalker` with the same arithmetic (the same bits and saved states). `WheeledGroup.advance(dt, intents, afterStep?)` takes a hook called after every step that may change the next step's intents; `WheeledGroup.fixed` is its `FixedSteps`, `stepSeconds` its step. `pnpm check:walk` checks frame rates against stepping by hand and the hook. See [The frame order](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/concepts.md#6-the-frame-order) and [Obstacles and other bodies](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/wheeled.md#obstacles-and-other-bodies).
- **An offset into the source of a buffer write** (in `gpu`): `Device.writeBuffer(buffer, data, count, offsetBytes?, srcOffset?)` skips `srcOffset` elements of `data` first (default 0, so existing calls write as before), also in what is kept to rebuild the buffer after a lost context. `ContactTrailLayer` and the bursts of `ParticleLayer` upload only the changed span of their rings with it.

### Changed

- **The movement example owns its numbers**: its follow camera uses `extraBoom`, `extraPivot` and `followHeading` instead of bookkeeping of its own and a read of the canvas's class (`__wh.getView().distance` is now the user's boom, as `setView` takes it, with `extraBoom` beside it); its cars take the hull box, the mass and the wheels' places from `COMPACT_CAR`, the measures their mesh is drawn to. The planet example keeps the movement profiles' slope effect for its walking figure, now documented in its README.
- **Getting on and off in the movement example is one mechanism**: the steed (`src/ride.ts`, `RIDE_SEAT`) and the cars (`src/car.ts`, `CAR_SEAT`) are two configurations of `SeatTransition` with their cues at times of it, their free side from `firstStandSpot`; the cars step through the group's hook instead of a loop of their own. The seat's weight is now an analytic ease of time (it no longer lags one frame) and the car's climb arc is a single eased rise; the scenarios hold their bounds.
- **The example `walk` is now `movement`** (`examples/movement`, its page at `/movement/`): it shows walking, riding an animal and driving cars. The checks keep their names (`tools/walk/`, `pnpm check:walk`).

## 0.8.0

### Added

- **Momentum of the walker**: `WalkerSetup.deceleration` (braking on the ground: a body at `runSpeed` v stops in v² / (2 deceleration) metres in the continuous limit, v · dt / 2 less in fixed steps; in the air every change is `airControl × acceleration`), `runAcceleration` (a slower run-up) and `turnSpeedAtRun` (the turn rate falls from `turnSpeed` at `walkSpeed` to this at `runSpeed`, and on the ground the velocity's direction turns no faster, so a runner turns in an arc). All three default to 0, and the default setup steps bit for bit as before. `Walker.retune(tuning)` and `ChartWalker.retune` change the speeds, rates and jump while the body lives (type `WalkerTuning`, `WALKER_TUNING_KEYS`). `pnpm check:walk` measures stop distances, the run-up and the turn's width. See [Momentum](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#momentum).
- **A chain that lags behind**: `ChainLag` (in `motion`, types `LagChain`, `ChainLagOptions`), secondary motion of a short chain of bones (a ponytail, a strap, a tail): a spring to the posed shape, damping against the joint, a share of gravity and an angle limit, in fixed steps; reset by a teleport, a stall restarts it moving with its joint; its velocity kept in a steady frame given apart from the solve's (a sphere's charts). Deterministic, no allocation per frame. `pnpm check:motion` checks rest, a swing that settles, the limit, bits, teleports and stalls, and a jumping solve frame. See [A chain that lags behind](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/motion.md#a-chain-that-lags-behind).
- **Movement profiles in the examples**: `default`, `heavy` and `light` (`examples/shared/src/figures/profiles.ts`), each a walker tuning and a gait's stride and lean. The shared gait leans into a run-up and back against braking (additive `push` and `brace` motions on the smoothed acceleration), and the pathfinder has a three-bone ponytail moved by `ChainLag`. The walk example draws the pathfinder by default (`figure=boxes` for the box figure) and the planet's walk mode carries the profile; both take `profile=` and cycle it with `P`; the planet's second figure walks heavy.
- **The diagonal of each quad of a sphere patch**: `SpherePatchOptions.split` (`'fixed'`, the default and the bytes as before, or `'shorter'`: per quad the diagonal whose ends differ less in height, so the edges of steep walls are no saw), the pure rule `spherePatchSplitsAD` and type `SpherePatchSplit` (in `gen-core`). `spherePatchRadius` and `sphereStripSurface` take the same `split`. See [A sphere world](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#a-sphere-world).
- **A category in the extra channel of a sphere patch**: `SpherePatchOptions.coarseExtra: 'nearest'` gives `a_coarseExtra` the bytes of the nearer parent vertex instead of a blend (default `'blend'`, unchanged).
- **The extra channel in the ground's shaders**: `SphereGroundOptions.extra` (type `SphereGroundExtra`, in `env`) makes `sphereGroundVs` read `a_extra` and `a_coarseExtra`, blend them like the colour and hand them on as `v_extra`; `extra.vertex` is a hook of the content, `groundVertex(p, extra)`, that moves the vertex before the cover's lift, in the colour and the shadow pass alike. Without `extra` both programs are unchanged. See [The ground of a sphere from patch cells](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#the-ground-of-a-sphere-from-patch-cells).
- **Walking on a sphere**: `ChartWalker` (in `world`, types `ChartWalkerOptions`, `RadiusAt`, `CHART_WALKER_STATE_BYTES`) runs the flat `CollisionWorld` and `Walker` unchanged in a moving local chart, `SurfaceChart` (in `base`, the exponential map round an anchor), recharted under the body at step boundaries with `onRechart(chart, world)` for the solids; deterministic and independent of how the time is cut into frames. `ChartFollowView` (in `camera`) shows a `FollowRig` that runs in the chart in world space with a render origin, and carries it across a rechart without moving the picture; its boom is cut by the chart's ground and solids. The planet example walks on the ground its cells draw (key `G`, `#walk=1`). See [Walking on a sphere](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#walking-on-a-sphere).
- **Solids of streamed cells**: `InstanceSolid` and `InstanceSolidBox` (in `base`), plain data per archetype that says where an instance record keeps its solid footprint (base, radius, height, factors, up `'y'` or `'radial'`, an optional turned box, `standable`, `blocksSight`, a minimum radius). `SolidIndex` (in `world`, with `newSolidHit`, types `SolidIndexOptions`, `SolidHit`, `SolidCellListener`) holds the solids of the cells added to it in world metres, in a spatial hash, and answers `within(point, radius, visit)` with position, radius, height, archetype, instance id and cell, without allocating, on a flat world and on a sphere. `SolidFeed` (in `world`, type `SolidFeedOptions`) keeps a `CollisionWorld` in step with it: once on a flat world, or from `ChartWalker`'s `onRechart` with the solids near the chart, kept up to date between recharts. `streamSolids(world, index, { level })` (in `runtime`, type `StreamSolidsOptions`) feeds the index from the resident cells of one level. The planet example's trees and rocks stop the walker and the camera's boom (shrubs do not); `__wh.solidsNear(radius)`. See [Solids of streamed cells](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/walking.md#solids-of-streamed-cells).
- **`CollisionWorld.query(x, z, radius, visit)`**: the solids within a radius of a point, with their ids, tags and signed distances.
- **Ends tilted with the ground**: `GroundFitOptions.align` (0–1, default 0: unchanged) makes `fitToGround` turn every chain's end bone about its joint by the ground's tilt under it, the normal taken from `heightAt` at `alignStep` (default 0.1 m) on either side, capped at `maxAlign` (default 0.6 rad). See [Motion](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/motion.md#reaching-and-the-ground).
- **Classes of channels**: `channelOrder(channels)` (the Strahler order of every channel), `channelFlow(channels, flow)` (type `ChannelFlow`: the gathered flow along every channel and the flow each hands on) and `classifyByThresholds(values, thresholds)` (how many ascending thresholds each value reaches), in `gen-plan`. Trace once from the smallest flow of interest and give each class its own width, profile or drawing; the rivers example draws brooks and streams narrower than its ground's grid this way. See [The plan of a sphere](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/generators.md#gen-plan-the-plan-of-a-sphere).
- **Reaching, aiming and holding over time** (in `motion`): `TwoBoneReach` (type `TwoBoneReachOptions`: a target set every frame, a weight eased in and out, a soft pull of a target out of reach so nothing pops, an optional pole and end rotation), `aimChain`, `aimAngles` and `ChainAim` (types `AimChain`, `AimAngles`: a short chain turned toward a point with a share per bone, yaw and pitch limits and no flip when the point passes behind), `socketTransform`, `blendTransform`, `SocketBlend` and `IDENTITY_34` (type `Socket`: a point on a bone, and a held thing moved between two sockets over set seconds), and `WeightFade`. The order in a frame is the ground fit, aims, reaches, sockets. The guide also shows an act of the arms over a gait with a mask and a layer, and a figure solved in a chart on a sphere. ### Changed

- **A streaming step costs less with many cells kept.** `StreamingManager.update` measures the distance of a cell to a ticket once in a step (the plan at two details, the keep band and the levels asked for it again), answers the keep band once for the children of one parent, and works out the level of a cell outside the plan only when the memory budget or the GPU ceiling asks for it. The actions of a step are the same as before.

### Fixed

- **Streaming comes to rest when the memory budget is tight.** When the wanted cells filled `memoryBudgetBytes`, the budget forgot the cells fetched ahead of need in the step they arrived and the next step asked for them again, for as long as the view stood: jobs ran without end and `StreamedWorld.settled` waited for ever (seen as hundreds of requests and evictions a second in a dense forest). A cell fetched only ahead of need is now requested only while it fits the budget beside the wanted and prefetched cells and the jobs in flight, and with selection by screen-space error the plan is kept within the budget as it is within `maxCells` and a GPU ceiling (`counters().detail`). With a budget that holds everything, nothing changes. See [The manager](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#the-manager).
- **Archetype meshes are uploaded once, not once per cell.** `StreamedWorld` uploaded the meshes of every level of detail of an archetype again for each cell that placed instances of it, so a forest of a few hundred cells held the same tree meshes hundreds of times (370 MB of cells where 85 MB do). The meshes are now uploaded on first use and shared; a cell uploads only its instance data. A cell's GPU bytes no longer include the archetype meshes; `Device.memory()` counts them once. See [`StreamedWorld`](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#streamedworld).
- **A GPU ceiling no longer makes the detail depend on the direction of view.** With selection by screen-space error and `gpuCeilingBytes` (`StreamingConfig.gpuBudgetBytes`), a plan that did not fit the room left by everything else on the GPU had its wanted cells deferred or taken off the GPU by urgency, which ranks the cells behind the viewer last. They stayed drawn by coarse ancestors, and the image never settled. On a sphere with a sea those ancestors lie under the water in low places, so a fast turn showed the sea where land should be. The plan is now kept within the room as well: the detail is lowered in every direction, by the same steps as for `maxCells`, and `counters().detail` reports it. Without a ceiling, or with a plan that fits, nothing changes. See [A ceiling of GPU memory](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#a-ceiling-of-gpu-memory).

### Changed

Breaking in this version:

- **Breaking: `Walker` no longer steers or turns in the air.** It keeps the horizontal velocity and the heading of take-off (after a jump or a fall from an edge) until it lands. `DEFAULT_WALKER.airControl` is 0 (was 0.3), and the new option `airTurnSpeed` (default 0) is the fastest turn of `yaw` in the air; before, `yaw` turned at `turnSpeed` in the air too. `{ airControl: 0.3, airTurnSpeed: 12 }` gives the old behaviour. - **Breaking (look): cloud shadows close under a closed deck.** Between a cover of 0.7 and 0.78 the gaps of the shadow pattern now close, so from 0.78 every place lies in the deck's shade; before, the ground lay in sunlit patches under a grey sky. This applies to the global cover, to the cover by place and to `cloudFieldSun` on the CPU.
- **Breaking (look): rain and snow fall in the world.** `createEngine({ precipitation: true })` in a perspective view no longer lays a pattern of streaks over the image: the drops and flakes are in the world round the eye (two ranges of world tiles to about 60 m, only the tiles in view), fall along the local down with the wind, end at the ground and behind walls (depth tested), and leave marks where they land on surfaces that face up within 30 m (splashes and, on wet ground, rings of rain; specks of snow). Nothing repeats and nothing moves with the eye. `precipitation` also takes `{ density, impacts, look }`; `look: 'screen'` brings back the old pattern, which orthographic views keep. With the marks on (the default) the standard depth mode draws offscreen (`readableDepth`): the same picture, about 0.15–0.3 ms more a frame even when nothing falls; `impacts: false` avoids it. The drops cost about 0.12–0.35 ms and the marks 0.25–0.35 ms at 1600 × 900 on an Apple M4 Pro. Under a roof it still rains.

Other changes:

- With `cloudVolume`, `createEngine({ sky })` makes the sky without its flat cloud deck: the volume draws the clouds. This follows only from the new option; a scene without `cloudVolume` keeps its deck.
- Raining clouds look like rain. Rain closes the sky over it (`cloudVolumeRainCover`; in GLSL also `cloudVolumeRainShape`), a raining deck reaches 85 % of the layer instead of 45 %, a raining cloud is up to three times as dense (`CLOUD_RAIN_DENSITY` is 2) and its light is greyer and darker. The cloud shell follows the same tops. A frame under rain costs about 0.2–0.4 ms more at 1600 × 900 on an Apple M4 Pro.
- `CloudShellLayer` costs far less where it covers the picture: the cover and the air between the eye and the shell are taken per vertex, only the pattern per fragment. Its part of a frame fell from about 2.5–4.9 ms to 0.6–1.3 ms below 10 km (Apple M4 Pro, 3456 × 2000).
- `CloudShellLayer` shows the pattern inside a closed deck too, and from below shades the base by what comes through the cloud instead of the sunlit top, so a deck seen from the ground is a grey sky with structure and no longer a flat white.
- `cloudLight` with the cover by place leaves out the pattern under a clear sky and under a closed deck, where it has no part in the result. The result is the same.

### Fixed

- `CloudVolumeLayer` no longer draws an opaque white band along the horizon when the eye is inside the cloud layer or just above it. A view ray that runs far through the layer now takes as many samples as keep the farthest within half a cell of the shape (up to four times `steps`), and the shape gives way to its mean only beyond that, so the horizon shows single clouds with sky between them. Views from the ground and from orbit do not change, and a frame costs the same within the spread.
- `CloudShellLayer` with the atmosphere slot lights the top of a cloud seen from above by the sun over its own place (`atmosphereSun`: white high in the day, red toward the night side), no longer by the frame's sun, which is the light at the eye. From orbit that turned whole decks pink. Without the slot nothing changes.
- Falling pieces (`fallPieces`) keep the openings of their part. The closed shell of a solid piece covered its whole outline, so a wall bay torn open by an earlier event (and a partition with a door, a slab with a stair hole) flew with the opening filled. A broad face of the shell now covers only what the piece's own surface parallel to it covers.

## 0.4.0

First public release. Earlier versions were never published.

### Added

- A WebGL2 engine for the browser in strict TypeScript: plain WebGL2, no graphics library, no loaded images. One world unit is one metre, y points up.
- One entry per module, `wallhammer-web/<module>`. The root entry re-exports `base`, `platform`, `core`, `gen-core`, `gen-mesh`, `gen-tex`, `gpu`, `camera`, `env`, `world` and `tools`.
- Modules in layers; a module imports only lower layers:
  - `base`: vector math, quaternions, the `View` contract, plain-data contracts for meshes, instances, textures, materials and bakes, the environment slot contract, destructible structure types, reproducible trigonometry and nested 64-bit frames (`FrameSet`).
  - Affine 3 × 4 matrices in `base` (`mat34Identity`, `mat34Multiply`, `mat34Invert` and others), 12 numbers column by column, the layout of an instance record.
  - `platform`: the only module that touches the browser. Canvas size, orbit, stepped, free and walk controls (keys, pointer, gamepad), the frame loop, workers, `fetchBytes`, a byte store in the browser's storage, loss of the graphics context.
  - `core`: `Clock`, the palette, ballistic tracks of falling pieces, orbits and spin as functions of time (`BodySystem`), level-of-detail fades.
  - `gen-core`: hash-based randomness with named streams, value noise, polygon geometry, spatial fields, instance cells, cell ids and world shapes for streaming, a cube blown up to a sphere, strips along a surface, small instances near the viewer.
  - `motion` (layer 2): skeletons and poses as data, blend and additive operations, world matrices (`solvePose`), a motion graph of states, transitions, layers, parameters, triggers and events at marks, `save` and `restore`, a two-bone reach and ends kept on the ground. It holds no motion of its own; every pose comes from a source the game writes. Not re-exported from the root entry.
  - `gen-mesh`: height fields, lines, cell surfaces with blending between levels, packed meshes, surfaces within an error tolerance, prisms, strips and spatial chunks.
  - The mesh builder in `gen-mesh`: `MeshBuilder` builds meshes in a vertex format the caller supplies, with a transform stack and mirroring. Bare shapes for it (`revolve`, `sweep`, `extrude`, `polygon`, `gridSurface`, `box`, `cylinder`) ask the caller for every vertex payload and carry no look of their own.
  - `gen-tex`: textures from code (`codeTexture`, `colorTable`, `boxBlur`).
  - `gen-plan`: coarse plans over a whole sphere: a grid of nodes, drainage, lines, least-cost routes and regions, picks of sites, plots along lines, the skyline of a height field.
  - Content modules `gen-arch` (structures from recipes, destructible descriptions, damage, interiors, rubble), `gen-ground` (ground bake from surface classes, season and snow slots) and `gen-vegetation` (placement, meshes, the sway slot). Nothing else in the engine depends on them.
  - `gpu`: the only module with WebGL2. `Device`, programs, buffers, textures, bakes, depth modes (standard, reversed, logarithmic), antialiasing, recovery from a lost context, GPU memory accounting.
  - `render`: `Renderer` with passes and shadow modes (single, snapped, cascades), moving shadow casters, mesh, piece, instanced, instance cell, instance set, transient, rig, particle, value and near-instance layers, point lights, shader includes.
  - Bone-driven instances in `render`: `BoneInstances` writes the bone matrices of a solved pose into instance set records without allocation. `RigLayer` can draw with the game's own material and cast shadows into the shadow map.
  - `camera`: `SteppedOrthoRig`, `PerspectiveOrbitRig` (can keep the eye above the ground), keyframed `OrbitPath`, `FreeRig`, `FollowRig`.
  - `env`: `Environment` with clocks, sun, light, weather, wind, clouds and fog, a fixed light mode, the sky, an atmosphere round a sphere, cloud cover by place.
  - `world`: picking, streaming without the GPU (`StreamingManager`, cell providers, the WHC1 cell file format, a cache, a GPU memory ceiling) and the sphere world shape (`SphereWorld`).
  - Walking in `world`: `CollisionWorld` (a base height function plus upright solids), a `Walker` that moves over it in fixed steps, saving and restoring the walker's state, collision solids from a mesh. Deterministic and runs in plain Node.
  - `sim`: a deterministic fixed-step simulation that runs in plain Node: commands, checkpoints, state hashes, entity tables, navigation, movers with collision modes, shared areas, agendas, state machines, region navigation, a flow network. `sim/destruction` applies events to destructible structures.
  - `runtime`: `createEngine`, `createHeadlessEngine`, `EnvironmentLink`, sky, atmosphere, precipitation and cloud shell layers, `StreamedWorld` with worker cell providers.
  - `tools`: the data view, the frame profile, a wireframe of collision solids, checks of built geometry.
- Documentation in the package (`docs/`): getting started, architecture, concepts, guides, the reference of every entry and recipes.
- The published package holds compiled JavaScript with type declarations (`dist/`). Its only runtime dependency is `earcut`. MIT licence.

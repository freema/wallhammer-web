# Changelog

All notable changes to the npm package `wallhammer-web` are recorded in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the package follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). While the version is 0.x, a minor version (for example 0.4 to 0.5) may contain breaking changes. A patch version does not.

## Unreleased

## 0.6.0

### Added

- **A painted sky**, opt-in: `AtmosphereParams.paint` (`AtmospherePaint`, `DEFAULT_ATMOSPHERE_PAINT`, in `env`) lays a gradient from the horizon to the zenith, a glow round the sun and a plain sun disc over the computed sky, gives the air in front of the scene the horizon's colour, and adds a haze that thins with height and may start some way from the eye (`hazeStartM`). Its values are read every frame; a slot made without it is unchanged. See [A painted sky](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#a-painted-sky).
- **A grade of the picture**: `createEngine({ grade })` and `Device.grade` (`DisplayGrade`, `DEFAULT_DISPLAY_GRADE`, in `gpu`) apply exposure, lift, gain, gamma, saturation and contrast when the frame is shown. See [A grade of the picture](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#a-grade-of-the-picture).
- **The look of the volumetric clouds**: `CloudVolumeLayer.style` (`CloudVolumeStyle`, `DEFAULT_CLOUD_VOLUME_STYLE`, in `runtime`) sets how crisp the clouds' edges are, how much they shade themselves, how evenly they scatter the sun and the colours of their lit and shaded sides. The default changes nothing.
- **A path for the free camera**: `FreePath` (in `camera`) samples keyframes of an eye in double precision and an aim, a look point or a heading and pitch in the local horizon of a caller's up (an up source, a function or a vector, so a sphere and a flat world alike), with optional roll and field of view, per-segment easing (`'smooth'`, `'linear'`, `'ease-in'`, `'ease-out'`, `'ease-in-out'`) and cuts. Catmull-Rom tangents keep the velocity continuous inside a group. `sample(t, out)` is pure, `ahead(t, horizonS, stepS)` samples coming poses, `apply(rig, t)` and `writeFreePose(rig, pose)` write into a `FreeRig`; `newFreePose`, types `FreeKey`, `FreePathEase`, `FreePathOptions`, `FreePathUp`, `FreePose`. `FreeRig.roll` turns the view about its direction (default 0), and `FreeRig.key()` now also changes with the field of view. See [Free paths](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/cameras.md#free-paths).
- **Rig motions on a clock**: `RigPart.rate` (cycles per second) runs a `spin`, `swing` or `bend` part on `RigLayer.time`, which the caller sets every frame, at full size whatever the speed, with the instance's seed as its phase (`rigClockPhase`, `rigClockRates`, `RIG_CLOCK_STEP`, `RIG_MAX_CLOCKS`). A `swing` may turn about y or z (`axis`, `RIG_SWING_KIND`). **A full frame per instance**: `RigDesc.orient: 'frame'` turns an instance by a forward and an up direction written with `writeRigFrame`, for bank and pitch of any size. Descriptions without these keep their program and mesh bytes. See [Motions on a clock and a full frame](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rigs.md#motions-on-a-clock-and-a-full-frame).
- **Preloading streamed cells along a path**: `preloadTickets(poses)` (in `runtime`, type `PreloadPose`) turns coming viewer poses into `preload` tickets; `StreamingManager.prefetched` and `StreamedWorld.preloaded` tell when everything fetched ahead of need is loaded as well as what is drawn. See [Preloading along a coming path](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#preloading-along-a-coming-path).
- **A cut to a preloaded place without a wait**: `StreamedWorld` option `uploadAhead` uploads the cells fetched ahead of need (preload tickets, the children to split next) within what is left of `uploadMsPerFrame` and keeps them on the GPU undrawn, so they are on the screen in the step a ticket wants them; `StreamingConfig.preloadBudgetBytes` gives the cells of preload tickets room of their own beside `memoryBudgetBytes`, so the place in view keeps its detail while a second one is held ready. `StreamingManager.ahead()` and `residentAll()` serve it. Both options are off by default and change nothing then. See [Preloading along a coming path](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#preloading-along-a-coming-path).
- **A liquid below the level of a sphere**: `LiquidSurface` (in `env`) raises waves of a wind on a chart of the sphere, gives the surface's height on the CPU and a frame's uniforms, and can loop for a scene that plays in a circle (`setLoop`); `liquidGlsl(look)`, `liquidRipplesGlsl()` and `liquidShaftsGlsl()` write the GLSL its materials share (types `LiquidLook`, `LiquidOptics`, `LiquidRipples`, `LiquidSurfaceOptions`, `LiquidState`, `LiquidShaftsOptions`; `LIQUID_RIPPLES`, `LIQUID_SIMPLEX_SKEW`, `liquidClearDistance`). `LiquidNearLayer`, `LiquidBackdropLayer`, `LiquidShaftsLayer` and `LiquidLensLayer` (in `runtime`, `LIQUID_SCENE_UNIT`) draw it round the eye. Moved from the `planet` example, which now uses it. See [A liquid below the level of a sphere](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#a-liquid-below-the-level-of-a-sphere).
- **Details streamed round the eye on a sphere**: `NearDetailStream` (in `runtime`) streams the records of `nearScatter` by square of a `NearSquareGrid` (in `gen-core`) and draws them with a `NearInstanceLayer`; `nearLatticeValue`, `nearPatch` and `nearSlopeDeg` read the ground of a square from a lattice. Moved from the `system` example, which now uses it. See [Details streamed round the eye](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#details-streamed-round-the-eye).
- **A largest slope for near instances**, opt-in: `NearScatterOptions.slope` leaves out the candidates on ground steeper than `maxDeg`; every other record keeps its bytes. See [Instances near the viewer](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/generators.md#instances-near-the-viewer).
- **The ground of a sphere from patch cells**: `sphereGroundVs`, `sphereGroundDepthVs`, `sphereCoverThinGlsl` and `DEPTH_ONLY_FS` (in `env`) are the vertex shaders of the colour and the shadow pass with hooks for the cover's lift (`SphereGroundOptions`); the content keeps its fragment shader. Moved from the `planet` example, which now uses them. See [The ground of a sphere from patch cells](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#the-ground-of-a-sphere-from-patch-cells).
- **A waterline without teeth**, opt-in: `SphereGroundVsOptions.waterline` with `SPHERE_GROUND_WATERLINE_GLSL` decides per fragment from its own height whether the ground lies under the level (`groundUnder`) and gives the alpha of the vertices above it alone (`groundLandAlpha`), instead of a test of the interpolated alpha. Without it the program is unchanged.
- **A scene that plays in a circle**: `loopWrap`, `loopClock`, `loopTurns` and `loopFade` (in `env`) bring a time into one pass, run an animation clock that closes with the loop, give a sine with a whole number of turns a pass and the share of a fade to the motion one pass earlier. See [A scene that plays in a circle](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#a-scene-that-plays-in-a-circle).

### Changed

- **A streaming step costs less with many cells kept.** `StreamingManager.update` measures the distance of a cell to a ticket once in a step (the plan at two details, the keep band and the levels asked for it again), answers the keep band once for the children of one parent, and works out the level of a cell outside the plan only when the memory budget or the GPU ceiling asks for it. The actions of a step are the same as before.

### Fixed

- **Streaming comes to rest when the memory budget is tight.** When the wanted cells filled `memoryBudgetBytes`, the budget forgot the cells fetched ahead of need in the step they arrived and the next step asked for them again, for as long as the view stood: jobs ran without end and `StreamedWorld.settled` waited for ever (seen as hundreds of requests and evictions a second in a dense forest). A cell fetched only ahead of need is now requested only while it fits the budget beside the wanted and prefetched cells and the jobs in flight, and with selection by screen-space error the plan is kept within the budget as it is within `maxCells` and a GPU ceiling (`counters().detail`). With a budget that holds everything, nothing changes. See [The manager](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#the-manager).
- **Archetype meshes are uploaded once, not once per cell.** `StreamedWorld` uploaded the meshes of every level of detail of an archetype again for each cell that placed instances of it, so a forest of a few hundred cells held the same tree meshes hundreds of times (370 MB of cells where 85 MB do). The meshes are now uploaded on first use and shared; a cell uploads only its instance data. A cell's GPU bytes no longer include the archetype meshes; `Device.memory()` counts them once. See [`StreamedWorld`](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#streamedworld).
- **A GPU ceiling no longer makes the detail depend on the direction of view.** With selection by screen-space error and `gpuCeilingBytes` (`StreamingConfig.gpuBudgetBytes`), a plan that did not fit the room left by everything else on the GPU had its wanted cells deferred or taken off the GPU by urgency, which ranks the cells behind the viewer last. They stayed drawn by coarse ancestors, and the image never settled. On a sphere with a sea those ancestors lie under the water in low places, so a fast turn showed the sea where land should be. The plan is now kept within the room as well: the detail is lowered in every direction, by the same steps as for `maxCells`, and `counters().detail` reports it. Without a ceiling, or with a plan that fits, nothing changes. See [A ceiling of GPU memory](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/streaming.md#a-ceiling-of-gpu-memory).

## 0.5.0

This version holds breaking changes (marked **Breaking**). Guides: [The environment](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md) (the table at the top of "Clouds" lists the pieces and what they cost) and [Rendering](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md).

### Added

- **Volumetric clouds**, opt-in: `createEngine({ cloudVolume: true })` (or `CloudVolumeOptions`) adds `CloudVolumeLayer` as `Engine.cloudVolume`. A layer of clouds with a body between two heights (1.5 and 4 km by default), seen from the ground, from inside and from above, on a sphere and on a flat world, with or without the atmosphere. Terrain in front hides them in every depth mode, and terrain inside the layer has clouds in front of it and none behind where the depth can be read. `quality` is `'low'`, `'medium'`, `'high'` or own numbers; `'medium'` costs about 0.9–1.6 ms at 3200 × 1800 on an Apple M4 Pro. `Engine.moveCloudsBefore(layer)` places the layer before the layers that blend over it. See [Volumetric clouds](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#volumetric-clouds).
- **One cloud field from orbit to the ground.** Under radial up `CloudShellLayer`, handed to the volume as its `shell`, draws the volume's own clouds: the volume near the eye, the shell beyond 20–40 km (`handoverFromM`, `handoverToM`) and all of them from 15–30 km up (`fadeFromM`, `fadeToM`). The hand-over changes the detail, not the clouds. On a flat world the shell keeps its pattern and the two cross-fade by height. See [The cloud shell](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#the-cloud-shell).
- **The cloud weather map**: `CloudWeatherMap` (in `runtime`) holds the cover, the deck share and the precipitation over every place, as a cube map under radial up and a tile of 64 km round the eye on a flat world. The deck share and the precipitation follow from the cover by `rules` (`CloudWeatherRules`, `DEFAULT_CLOUD_WEATHER_RULES`; `cloudWeatherKind` on the CPU). The volume makes its own map (`CloudVolumeLayer.weather`): a deck flattens into the lower part of the layer, raining clouds grow to its top. See [The cloud weather map](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#the-cloud-weather-map).
- **Rain under the clouds and rain shafts.** `CloudVolumeLayer.rainShare` is the share of the frame's precipitation that falls under the column over the eye, and `PrecipitationLayer.source` (set by `createEngine`) scales the rain and snow by it, so the rain stops under a gap. With the eye under the base the volume draws grey rain shafts under raining clouds (`rainShafts`, 1 by default, 0 off), up to 30 km away.
- **Cloud shadows from a texture**, opt-in: `createEngine({ cloudShadows: true })` (or `{ extentM, size, interval }`) adds `cloudShadowSlot()` and `CloudShadowLayer` as `Engine.cloudShadows`. The share of the sun is drawn every fourth frame into a texture of 512² texels over a square of 24 cloud sizes round the eye, seen along the light and snapped to the world; `cloudLight` makes one lookup there in the place of its noise. With `cloudVolume` the texture holds the shadows of the volume's clouds (`CloudShadowLayer.source`, `CloudShadowSource`). In the `system` example a frame fell from 7.4–7.9 ms to 4.8–5.1 ms below 10 km (Apple M4 Pro, 3200 × 1800). See [Cloud shadows from a texture](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#cloud-shadows-from-a-texture).
- **A clear medium below a level**, in `core`: `mediumPath` (the part of a segment below the level), `mediumThrough`, `mediumClearDistance`, `mediumFresnel` (with total reflection from the denser side), and `MEDIUM_GLSL` with `mediumPath`, `mediumThrough`, `mediumFresnel` and `mediumApply`. They take heights relative to the level, so they serve a radial world and a flat one. The `planet` example builds its sea with them. See [A medium below a level](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#a-medium-below-a-level).
- **A moving surface of waves**, in `gen-core`: `waveTrains` makes up to eight trochoidal (Gerstner) trains from a wind and a seed; `waveDisplace`, `waveNormal` (with the Jacobian, for foam on the crests), `waveHeight`, `waveFade` and `waveUnresolved` evaluate them, each train faded by the size of a sample so nothing aliases. `WAVES_GLSL` holds the same functions for shaders, fed by `waveUniforms`, which computes the phases at an origin in double. All of it is a pure function of the trains, the point and the time (types `WaveTrain`, `WaveFieldOptions`; `WAVE_TRAINS_MAX`, `WAVE_GRAVITY`). The `planet` example moves its sea with it. See [A moving surface of waves](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#a-moving-surface-of-waves).
- **Noise volumes**: `NoiseVolumes` (in `render`) makes a tileable Perlin-Worley shape volume (128³) and a Worley detail volume (32³) on the GPU from a seed. `gen-tex` holds their recipe and the same texel functions on the CPU (`noiseVolumeData`, `shapeNoiseAt`, `detailNoiseAt`, `worleyTile`, `perlinTile`); the two sides agree to within one step of 8 bits. See [Noise volumes](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/rendering.md#noise-volumes).
- **3D textures and cube maps as draw targets** in `gpu`: `Device.volumeTarget`, `cubeTarget`, `fillLayer`, `volumeTexture`, `cubeTexture`, `deleteLayeredTarget`; types `VolumeTarget`, `CubeTarget`, `VolumeFormat`.
- **The ground follows the weather**, opt-in: `weatherGround(weather, dtS, rates?)` (in `env`) returns the weather after a step of time with snowfall raising `snowCover`, rain raising `wetness` and washing the snow away, and the ground drying (and with `thawPerS` thawing) while nothing falls; pure, within 0–1, stepped with the app's clock. `DEFAULT_WEATHER_GROUND` covers the ground after three minutes of heavy snow, wets it within 20 s of rain and dries it over four minutes. The minimal example shows it with `ground=follow`.

Smaller additions:

- `gpu` and `render`: `Device.sceneDepth()` copies the depth of the main target drawn so far into a texture (reversed and logarithmic depth, or two samples). `Device.drawVertices(count)` draws triangles without vertex data. `Device.deleteColorTarget`. `Renderer.projection` is the depth projection of the frame being drawn.
- `gpu` and `render`: `RendererOptions.readableDepth` and `Device.setReadableDepth(on)` let the standard depth mode draw offscreen so its depth can be read; `createContext(canvas, depth, samples, readableDepth)` makes the canvas to match. The picture is the same; the copy costs about 0.15–0.3 ms a frame at 1600 × 900.
- The model of rain and snow on the CPU and in GLSL (`env`): `PRECIPITATION_GLSL`, `precipitationDown`, `precipitationVelocity`, `precipitationCount`, `precipitationTiles`, `precipitationClock`, `precipitationDrop`, `precipitationEyeOffset`, `precipitationImpactClock`, `precipitationImpactAt`, `RAIN_FALL`, `SNOW_FALL`, `RAIN_SHELLS`, `SNOW_SHELLS`, `RAIN_SPLASHES`, `RAIN_RINGS`, `SNOW_SPECKS`, `PRECIPITATION_MAX_TILES`, `PRECIPITATION_IMPACT_PERIOD_M`, `PRECIPITATION_MIN` and their types; in `runtime` `precipitationNeedsDepth`, `PrecipitationOptions`, `PRECIP_DROP_VS`, `PRECIP_DROP_FS`, `PRECIP_IMPACT_FS`, and `Engine.precipitation` is public.
- `env`: `lightingGlsl` and `shadowGlsl` take `{ cloudShadow: true }` for the variant of `cloudLight` that reads the cloud shadow texture (the environment link registers it when the block holds the slot). `atmosphereOvercast`, the atmosphere's param `cloudTopM`, the value `AtmosphereValues.overcast` and the Frame member `u_atmCloud` (see "Changed"). `cloudShadowFillFs({ cloudField, depth })` and `cloudWeatherFillFs({ cloudField })` are the fragment shaders that fill the cloud shadow texture and the weather map; `CLOUD_WEATHER_GLSL` reads the map.
- `runtime`: `SkyLayer` takes `{ clouds: false }`, which leaves out the flat cloud deck. `AtmosphereLayer.bindTables(device)` binds the air's tables for a layer that reads them in its own `prepare`.
- `CloudVolumeLayer`: `shape`, `quality`, `enabled`, `shell` (a `CloudFarLayer`, whose `volume` the layer sets in every frame), `weight`, `fadeFromM`, `fadeToM`, `handoverFromM`, `handoverToM`, `maxDistanceM`, `history`, `frameIndex`, `resetHistory()`, `rainShare`, `rainShafts`, `shadowSamples`, `noise`, `weather`, `bindField(device)` and `unbindField(device)`. By default it keeps its clouds from frame to frame (`history`); `history = false` with `frameIndex` gives a frame of its own for tests.
- `CloudShadowLayer`: `enabled` (false computes the share per fragment again), `interval`, `invalidate()`, `texture`, `source`, `slot`. `CloudWeatherMap`: `rules`, `interval`, `bind(device, unit)`, `tile`, `cube`.
- `gen-tex`: `NoiseVolumeRecipe` with `low`, the lowest value the shape's Perlin-Worley mix is stretched from.
- The pieces of the cloud model on the CPU and in GLSL, for tests and own layers:
  - the volume (`env`): `CLOUD_VOLUME_GLSL`, `CloudVolumeShape`, `DEFAULT_CLOUD_VOLUME_SHAPE`, `CloudVolumeQuality`, `CLOUD_VOLUME_QUALITY`, `cloudVolumeDensity` (takes the cloud type), `cloudVolumeProfile`, `cloudVolumeTypedProfile`, `cloudVolumeDeck`, `cloudVolumeThreshold`, `cloudVolumeSpan`, `cloudVolumeFade`, `cloudVolumeNearShare`, `cloudVolumeRainCover`, `cloudVolumeColumnDepth`, `cloudVolumeRainShare`, `CLOUD_RAIN_DENSITY`;
  - its march and history: `cloudVolumeMarchSize`, `cloudVolumeTargetSize`, `cloudVolumeTiles`, `CLOUD_VOLUME_TILE`, `cloudVolumeJitter`, `cloudVolumeBayer`, `cloudVolumeScreenBasis`, `cloudVolumeDepthDistance`, `cloudVolumeDepthMap`, `cloudVolumeReproject`, `cloudVolumeKeepHistory`;
  - the cloud shadow texture: `cloudShadowProjection`, `CloudShadowOptions`, `CloudShadowParams`, `CloudShadowValues`, `CLOUD_SHADOW_SLOT`, `CLOUD_SHADOW_UNIT`, `CLOUD_SHADOW_SIZE`, `CLOUD_SHADOW_EXTENT_CLOUDS`; the weather map: `CloudWeatherMapOptions`;
  - the noise volumes' recipe: `NOISE_VOLUME_SHAPE`, `NOISE_VOLUME_DETAIL`, `NOISE_SEED_PERLIN`, `NOISE_SEED_WORLEY`, `NOISE_SEED_DETAIL`, `PERLIN_GRADIENTS`, `WORLEY_WEIGHTS`, `octaveSeed`, `worleyOctaves`, `NOISE_VOLUME_GLSL`, `NoiseVolumesOptions`.

### Changed

Breaking in this version:

- **Breaking: `Walker` no longer steers or turns in the air.** It keeps the horizontal velocity and the heading of take-off (after a jump or a fall from an edge) until it lands. `DEFAULT_WALKER.airControl` is 0 (was 0.3), and the new option `airTurnSpeed` (default 0) is the fastest turn of `yaw` in the air; before, `yaw` turned at `turnSpeed` in the air too. `{ airControl: 0.3, airTurnSpeed: 12 }` gives the old behaviour. A step that starts with the feet on the ground (also the first step after `teleport` onto the ground) steers as on the ground.
- **Breaking (types): `AtmosphereParams.cloudTopM` is a new required field** (default 4000 in `DEFAULT_ATMOSPHERE`). Code that passes a full `AtmosphereParams` literal, for example to `computeAtmosphere`, must add it. `createEngine({ atmosphere })` and `atmosphereSlot` take partial params and need no change.
- **Breaking (look): overcast light with the atmosphere.** Every scene with the atmosphere and a cloud cover over the eye above 0.5 looks different, with no opt-in: the slot carries how overcast the sky over the eye is (`atmosphereOvercast`: the cover from 0.5 to 0.9, fading out above the clouds' top `cloudTopM`), `atmosphereLight` then lights surfaces with the grey, even light the clouds pass on, and `atmosphereApply` greys and dims the air. See [Weather](packages/wallhammer-web/docs/environment.md#weather).
- **Breaking (look): cloud shadows close under a closed deck.** Between a cover of 0.7 and 0.78 the gaps of the shadow pattern now close, so from 0.78 every place lies in the deck's shade; before, the ground lay in sunlit patches under a grey sky. This applies to the global cover, to the cover by place and to `cloudFieldSun` on the CPU.
- **Breaking (look): rain and snow fall in the world.** `createEngine({ precipitation: true })` in a perspective view no longer lays a pattern of streaks over the image: the drops and flakes are in the world round the eye (two ranges of world tiles to about 60 m, only the tiles in view), fall along the local down with the wind, end at the ground and behind walls (depth tested), and leave marks where they land on surfaces that face up within 30 m (splashes and, on wet ground, rings of rain; specks of snow). Nothing repeats and nothing moves with the eye. `precipitation` also takes `{ density, impacts, look }`; `look: 'screen'` brings back the old pattern, which orthographic views keep. With the marks on (the default) the standard depth mode draws offscreen (`readableDepth`): the same picture, about 0.15–0.3 ms more a frame even when nothing falls; `impacts: false` avoids it. The drops cost about 0.12–0.35 ms and the marks 0.25–0.35 ms at 1600 × 900 on an Apple M4 Pro. Under a roof it still rains.

Other changes:

- With `cloudVolume`, `createEngine({ sky })` makes the sky without its flat cloud deck: the volume draws the clouds. This follows only from the new option; a scene without `cloudVolume` keeps its deck.
- Raining clouds look like rain. Rain closes the sky over it (`cloudVolumeRainCover`; in GLSL also `cloudVolumeRainShape`), a raining deck reaches 85 % of the layer instead of 45 %, a raining cloud is up to three times as dense (`CLOUD_RAIN_DENSITY` is 2) and its light is greyer and darker. The cloud shell follows the same tops. A frame under rain costs about 0.2–0.4 ms more at 1600 × 900 on an Apple M4 Pro.
- `CloudShellLayer` costs far less where it covers the picture: the cover and the air between the eye and the shell are taken per vertex, only the pattern per fragment. Its part of a frame fell from about 2.5–4.9 ms to 0.6–1.3 ms below 10 km (Apple M4 Pro, 3456 × 2000).
- `CloudShellLayer` shows the pattern inside a closed deck too, and from below shades the base by what comes through the cloud instead of the sunlit top, so a deck seen from the ground is a grey sky with structure and no longer a flat white.
- `cloudLight` with the cover by place leaves out the pattern under a clear sky and under a closed deck, where it has no part in the result. The result is the same.

### Fixed

- `CloudVolumeLayer` no longer draws an opaque white band along the horizon when the eye is inside the cloud layer or just above it. A view ray that runs far through the layer now takes as many samples as keep the farthest within half a cell of the shape (up to four times `steps`), and the shape gives way to its mean only beyond that, so the horizon shows single clouds with sky between them. Views from the ground and from orbit do not change, and a frame costs the same within the spread.
- `CloudShellLayer` with the atmosphere slot lights the top of a cloud seen from above by the sun over its own place (`atmosphereSun`: white high in the day, red toward the night side), no longer by the frame's sun, which is the light at the eye. From orbit that turned whole decks pink. Without the slot nothing changes.
- Falling pieces (`fallPieces`) keep the openings of their part. The closed shell of a solid piece covered its whole outline, so a wall bay torn open by an earlier event (and a partition with a door, a slab with a stair hole) flew with the opening filled. A broad face of the shell now covers only what the piece's own surface parallel to it covers.

## 0.4.0

First public release. Earlier versions were never published.

### Added

- A WebGL2 engine for the browser in strict TypeScript: plain WebGL2, no graphics library, no loaded images. One world unit is one metre, y points up.
- One entry per module, `wallhammer-web/<module>`. The root entry re-exports `base`, `platform`, `core`, `gen-core`, `gen-mesh`, `gen-tex`, `gpu`, `camera`, `env`, `world` and `tools`.
- Modules in layers; a module imports only lower layers:
  - `base`: vector math, quaternions, the `View` contract, plain-data contracts for meshes, instances, textures, materials and bakes, the environment slot contract, destructible structure types, reproducible trigonometry and nested 64-bit frames (`FrameSet`).
  - Affine 3 × 4 matrices in `base` (`mat34Identity`, `mat34Multiply`, `mat34Invert` and others), 12 numbers column by column, the layout of an instance record.
  - `platform`: the only module that touches the browser. Canvas size, orbit, stepped, free and walk controls (keys, pointer, gamepad), the frame loop, workers, `fetchBytes`, a byte store in the browser's storage, loss of the graphics context.
  - `core`: `Clock`, the palette, ballistic tracks of falling pieces, orbits and spin as functions of time (`BodySystem`), level-of-detail fades.
  - `gen-core`: hash-based randomness with named streams, value noise, polygon geometry, spatial fields, instance cells, cell ids and world shapes for streaming, a cube blown up to a sphere, strips along a surface, small instances near the viewer.
  - `motion` (layer 2): skeletons and poses as data, blend and additive operations, world matrices (`solvePose`), a motion graph of states, transitions, layers, parameters, triggers and events at marks, `save` and `restore`, a two-bone reach and ends kept on the ground. It holds no motion of its own; every pose comes from a source the game writes. Not re-exported from the root entry.
  - `gen-mesh`: height fields, lines, cell surfaces with blending between levels, packed meshes, surfaces within an error tolerance, prisms, strips and spatial chunks.
  - The mesh builder in `gen-mesh`: `MeshBuilder` builds meshes in a vertex format the caller supplies, with a transform stack and mirroring. Bare shapes for it (`revolve`, `sweep`, `extrude`, `polygon`, `gridSurface`, `box`, `cylinder`) ask the caller for every vertex payload and carry no look of their own.
  - `gen-tex`: textures from code (`codeTexture`, `colorTable`, `boxBlur`).
  - `gen-plan`: coarse plans over a whole sphere: a grid of nodes, drainage, lines, least-cost routes and regions, picks of sites, plots along lines, the skyline of a height field.
  - Content modules `gen-arch` (structures from recipes, destructible descriptions, damage, interiors, rubble), `gen-ground` (ground bake from surface classes, season and snow slots) and `gen-vegetation` (placement, meshes, the sway slot). Nothing else in the engine depends on them.
  - `gpu`: the only module with WebGL2. `Device`, programs, buffers, textures, bakes, depth modes (standard, reversed, logarithmic), antialiasing, recovery from a lost context, GPU memory accounting.
  - `render`: `Renderer` with passes and shadow modes (single, snapped, cascades), moving shadow casters, mesh, piece, instanced, instance cell, instance set, transient, rig, particle, value and near-instance layers, point lights, shader includes.
  - Bone-driven instances in `render`: `BoneInstances` writes the bone matrices of a solved pose into instance set records without allocation. `RigLayer` can draw with the game's own material and cast shadows into the shadow map.
  - `camera`: `SteppedOrthoRig`, `PerspectiveOrbitRig` (can keep the eye above the ground), keyframed `OrbitPath`, `FreeRig`, `FollowRig`.
  - `env`: `Environment` with clocks, sun, light, weather, wind, clouds and fog, a fixed light mode, the sky, an atmosphere round a sphere, cloud cover by place.
  - `world`: picking, streaming without the GPU (`StreamingManager`, cell providers, the WHC1 cell file format, a cache, a GPU memory ceiling) and the sphere world shape (`SphereWorld`).
  - Walking in `world`: `CollisionWorld` (a base height function plus upright solids), a `Walker` that moves over it in fixed steps, saving and restoring the walker's state, collision solids from a mesh. Deterministic and runs in plain Node.
  - `sim`: a deterministic fixed-step simulation that runs in plain Node: commands, checkpoints, state hashes, entity tables, navigation, movers with collision modes, shared areas, agendas, state machines, region navigation, a flow network. `sim/destruction` applies events to destructible structures.
  - `runtime`: `createEngine`, `createHeadlessEngine`, `EnvironmentLink`, sky, atmosphere, precipitation and cloud shell layers, `StreamedWorld` with worker cell providers.
  - `tools`: the data view, the frame profile, a wireframe of collision solids, checks of built geometry.
- Documentation in the package (`docs/`): getting started, architecture, concepts, guides, the reference of every entry and recipes.
- The published package holds compiled JavaScript with type declarations (`dist/`). Its only runtime dependency is `earcut`. MIT licence.
